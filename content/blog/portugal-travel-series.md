## Porto, Portugal 🇵🇹

There are some cities you visit… and then there are cities you slowly sink into.
Porto felt exactly like that.

Located in the Northwest Portugal, along the Duoro River, Porto is the country’s 2nd largest city.
It played an important part in Portugal’s identity.
A flourishing trading port for centuries, Porto’s shot to fame came by exporting Port wine to the world, in the 18th century.
While Porto is still famous for its wine, there are a ton of other reasons why Porto is a must visit travel destination.
From its stunning old town , to its authentic cuisine, and romantic viewpoints, Porto has so much to offer .

##  🎬 Day 1 - Exploring the Best of Porto 
##Capela das Almas - Our first stop was the beautiful Capela das Almas.
Covered in thousands of blue azulejo tiles, this chapel, also known as the Chapel of Souls,  instantly felt like the perfect introduction to the city.
The blue-and-white panels here depict various historic scenes.

![Blue azulejo tiles, Porto](/images/blogs/portugal/blue-tiles-porto.jpeg)

![Azulejos in Porto](/images/blogs/portugal/porto-azelujos.jpeg)


A Brasileira Café - 
Just a short walk away, we stopped at the historic A Brasileira café.
One thing I quickly realized in Portugal — even the simplest bakery items somehow taste unforgettable.
And the Bolos de Arroz… soft, warm, slightly crisp on the outside… felt like the perfect slow Porto morning.

Conga's Bifana Sandwich - No trip to Porto feels complete without trying a Bifana.
So next, we headed to Conga.
Simple place. No unnecessary hype.
Just a spicy, juicy sandwich packed with flavor.
Not every great travel memory needs a palace or a cathedral.
And sometimes… they come from  holding a delicious sandwich.

Porto Cathedral - From there, we climbed up toward Porto Cathedral.
And this is where Porto really began to reveal itself.
The orange rooftops. The river. The layered streets. The bridges.
Porto doesn’t feel polished. And that’s exactly what makes it beautiful.
It feels lived in. Real. Timeless.

Dom Luís I Bridge - This is one of Porto’s most iconic experiences — walking across the upper level of the Dom Luís I Bridge.
And honestly… this isn’t just a bridge. It’s the heartbeat of the city.
On one side — Porto. On the other — Vila Nova de Gaia.
And below, the Douro River quietly flowing through it all.

![Dom Luís I Bridge, Porto](/images/blogs/portugal/dum-luis-porto.jpeg)


Jardim do Morro Sunset - By evening, we reached Jardim do Morro.
This might just be one of the best sunset spots in all of Portugal.
As the sky slowly turned gold, the entire city seemed to glow.
Locals sat on the grass. Street musicians played nearby. And for a moment… nobody seemed in a hurry.
That’s something Porto does really well.


Francesinha at Café Santiago - And finally… it was time for Porto’s legendary Francesinha. Which honestly feels less like a sandwich… and more like a challenge.
Layers of meat, melted cheese, rich sauce, fries — all somehow coming together into one incredibly comforting meal.
Heavy? Absolutely. Worth it? Without question.

##  🎬 Day 2 - Porto Has Much More To Offer
São Bento Station - The next morning started at São Bento Station.
And this place completely exceeded expectations.
What looks like an ordinary train station from outside suddenly opens into walls covered with thousands of azulejo tiles.
Scenes of battles. History. Daily life.
It almost feels less like a station… and more like a museum people casually pass through every day.

Clérigos Tower - From there, we explored the Clérigos area 


Igreja do Carmo - Just nearby sits Igreja do Carmo — another reminder that in Porto, even churches feel like works of art.
Everywhere you look, there’s texture. Color. History.
Porto rewards slow walking.

Livraria Lello - We also stopped by Livraria Lello… but the line was honestly intense.
And while the bookstore is beautiful, the whole ‘Harry Potter inspiration’ debate still feels a little… tourist-marketing-ish.
So instead of spending hours in line, we decided to keep exploring Porto itself.”

The Famous McDonald’s - And yes… Porto is also home to what many call the world’s most beautiful McDonald’s.
Only in Europe can fast food somehow feel historic.


Ribeira Waterfront
Eventually, we made our way down into Ribeira.
And this is probably the Porto most people dream about.
Colorful riverside buildings. Outdoor cafés. Boats floating along the Douro.
It feels lively… but never overwhelming.


Douro River Cruise - One of the best ways to experience Porto is from the water.
So we hopped onto the famous Six Bridges Cruise.
And slowly passing beneath each bridge gave an entirely different perspective of the city.
From the river, Porto somehow feels even more cinematic.

Vila Nova de Gaia — Port Wine
Across the bridge sits Vila Nova de Gaia — home of Porto’s legendary Port wine cellars. And even if you’re not a wine expert, the atmosphere here is worth experiencing. Sunset by the river. Wine glasses clinking. Boats drifting past.
It’s one of those evenings you wish lasted longer.

Gazela — Cachorrinho
Before leaving Porto, there was still one final stop. Gazela.
Home of Porto’s famous Cachorrinho. Crispy bread. Spicy sausage. Melted cheese.
Simple. Messy. Perfect.

Through the sounds of the river. The blue tiles. The bridges. The sunsets. The food.
Porto Grows on You. And somewhere between all the walking Porto begins to feel less like a destination… and more like a feeling.

We hopped to a FlixBus and reached Portugal's capital at night. The public transport expeirence was very very smooth. No complains. You can also take the CP Trains.

## Why Lisbon is a must-visit destination 🇵🇹
First off, Lisbon’s rich history is on full display. From the imposing Castelo de São Jorge, overlooking the entire city, to the UNESCO World Heritage Site of Jerónimos Monastery, you’ll be tripping over centuries of fascinating stories.
Then there’s the food. Oh, the food! Whether you’re munching on a pastel de nata  or feasting on piri peri chicken at Rossio, or trying the ginginha your taste buds are in for a treat, there’s really something for every budget. 

The beauty of Lisbon is you can spend a little or a lot of time there and you will be certain to have a fantastic time.

##  🎬 🇵🇹 Day 3 — Alfama, Castles & Sunsets

If there’s one thing that instantly screams Lisbon… it’s Tram 28.
So naturally, we started our Lisbon adventure from Martim Moniz aboard the city’s iconic yellow tram. And honestly… calling this public transport feels generous.
This is basically a moving sightseeing tour disguised as a tram.
As it rattles through Lisbon’s steep hills and impossibly narrow lanes, the city slowly starts unfolding around you.

São Jorge Castle & Alfama - From there, we climbed toward São Jorge Castle.
And yes… your legs will absolutely feel it. But Lisbon rewards effort with views.
And from up here, the entire city stretches out toward the Tagus River in layers of terracotta rooftops and church domes. What makes Alfama special isn’t just the monuments. 
It’s the atmosphere. You’re not really supposed to “see” Alfama. You’re supposed to wander through it. Get lost.Take random turns. And somehow end up discovering tiny hidden corners that feel frozen in time.

Miradouro das Portas do Sol - One thing Lisbon takes very seriously… is viewpoints.
And the views from Portas do Sol genuinely feel postcard-worthy.
At some point we realized: in Lisbon, every uphill walk ends with somebody saying, “Wait… this might actually be the best view in the city.”

Praça do Comércio & Rua Augusta - Eventually, we made our way downhill toward Praça do Comércio. Bright yellow buildings. Open riverfront views. Street performers everywhere.This is the kind of place where you sit down for “just five minutes”…and accidentally stay for forty-five.

Rua Augusta Arch - Situated in Praça do Comércio, this majestic arch stands as a triumph of resilience, commemorating the city's rapid recovery and reconstruction after the devastating 1755 earthquake. From there, we walked through Rua Augusta — probably one of the liveliest streets in Lisbon. But somehow Lisbon still feels relaxed underneath all the energy.

Santa Justa Lift & Carmo Convent - Next came the famous Santa Justa Lift.
And yes the line can get really long .. well for us it was closed during our visit. 
But even just seeing it from below feels impressive. Only Europe can casually place a neo-Gothic elevator in the middle of downtown and act like it’s completely normal. Just nearby sits the ruins of Carmo Convent — one of the city’s most hauntingly beautiful places. Destroyed during the massive 1755 earthquake, the roofless arches somehow make the place feel even more powerful.
Lisbon doesn’t hide its history. It lives alongside it.


Piri Piri Chicken & Ginjinha - By afternoon it was finally time for food.
And Portugal seriously knows how to make simple food unforgettable.
Spicy piri piri chicken.Fresh bread. Cold drinks.Tiny restaurants packed with energy.
And of course… Ginjinha. Lisbon’s famous cherry liqueur.

Sunset at Senhora do Monte - 
To end the day, we headed up to Miradouro da Senhora do Monte for sunset.
And somehow… Lisbon saved one of its best views for last.
As the city slowly turned gold beneath us, you could hear tram bells echoing through the hills below. And in that moment, Lisbon didn’t just feel beautiful. 

It felt alive.

##  🎬 🇵🇹 Day 4 — Belém & Fado
Tram 15E to Belém - The next morning, we took Tram 15E toward Belém.
And if Alfama feels like Lisbon’s soul…Belém feels like its history book.
This entire area is tied to Portugal’s Age of Discovery — when explorers once sailed from these shores into the unknown.

Pastéis de Belém - But before any history lessons…priorities.
Pastéis de Belém. Warm custard tarts. Flaky pastry.Cinnamon on top.
There’s a reason people queue here every single day.
And honestly , one bite in… and the queue suddenly makes complete sense.

Jerónimos Monastery - 
Just nearby stands the breathtaking Jerónimos Monastery.
And the level of detail here is honestly ridiculous.
The stonework almost looks soft — like somebody carved lace into rock.
Portugal really chose maximum drama when designing its monuments.

Monument to the Discoveries & Belém Tower - 
Next came the Monument to the Discoveries and the iconic Belém Tower.
And standing here beside the Tagus River, it’s easy to imagine the scale of Portugal’s maritime history. Also… the nearby bridge looks suspiciously like San Francisco’s Golden Gate Bridge. Portugal clearly decided , “if it works, it works.”

Rossio, Graça Funicular & Alfama Evening - Back in central Lisbon, we wandered through Rossio Square before riding the newly reopened Graça Funicular.
And honestly… Lisbon somehow found a way to make public transport cinematic.
Again. At the top, the views from Graça felt quieter.Less chaotic.More local.
One of the best things about Lisbon is that every neighborhood somehow feels like its own little world.

Free Fado at A Tasca do Chico - To end the night, we headed to A Tasca do Chico for Fado. Even if you don’t understand Portuguese…you somehow understand the feeling.
And this is where Lisbon slows down. No flashy production.No giant stage.
Just music filled with emotion, longing, and stories.
Even if you don’t understand Portuguese…you somehow understand the feeling.
And honestly, that’s what makes Fado so powerful.

##  🎬 🇵🇹 Day 5 — Sintra & The Coast
If you’re visiting Lisbon and have one extra day… this might just be the perfect escape. The picturesque town of Sintra located 30 kilometres to the west of Lisbon, is home to ancient castles, extravagant palaces, and beautiful natural scenery.
In this video, we'll show you how to visit Sintra as a day trip from Lisbon.
The cheapest way to get to Sintra is by train. The train leaves from the Rossio  station in downtown Lisbon . It takes about 45 minutes to get to Sintra.
It's best to leave as early as possible to make the most of your day.

The moment you arrive in Sintra, you immediately realize two things.
First — this place is absolutely beautiful. And second — your legs are about to get tested.

## Pena Palace

![Pena Palace, Sintra](/images/blogs/portugal/pena-sintra.jpeg)

![Sintra, Portugal](/images/blogs/portugal/Sintra.jpeg)
This colourful, eccentric palace perched atop a hill is the poster child of Sintra, it’s the definition of Instagram worthy. Its vibrant yellows and reds make it look like a child’s crayon drawing come to life. The views from up here are spectacular – on a clear day, you can see all the way to Lisbon!

## Quinta da Regaleira
Now, it's upto your schedule if you want to all the three major attractions of Sintra.
Pena Palace goes without saying is a must visit. With our limited schedule we opted out of the recently popular site - Quinta da Regaleira. 
Now, don't get me wrong with Quinta da Regaleira. This estate is a wonderland of grottos, wells, and gardens.Don’t miss the Initiation Well, a spiral staircase sinking deep into the earth. It’s equal parts beautiful and slightly creepy, in the best possible way!

But we wanted to balance out our last day between Sintra and the Atlantic Coast.
So hang on tight as I narrate you our journey.

## Castle of the Moors
After Pena Palace we opted for the Castle of the Moors.
The millenia old Castle walls provided just the dramatic hill shots we were looking for. And walking these ancient stone walls high above Sintra feels completely different from Lisbon.

## Lunch at Sintra town centre
Try the local speciality, Travesseiros – flaky pastries filled with almond cream, take some time to explore the town centre. It’s a picturesque place with narrow streets, quaint shops, and more palaces than you can shake a stick at.

We then hopped on a Bolt to the beautiful Atlantic Coast , which is just 15 mins away.

A tiny whitewashed village sitting dramatically above the ocean.
As the sun slowly disappeared into the ocean, everything became quiet.
Just cliffs, waves, golden light… and that strange feeling you get when a trip turns into a memory before it’s even over.

## FOOD

![Francesinha in Porto](/images/blogs/portugal/francesinha-porto.jpeg)
No trip to Lisbon is complete without indulging in the local cuisine. Here are some must-tries:
* Pastéis de Nata: These custard tarts are Lisbon’s most famous treat. Try them at Pastéis de Belém for the original recipe.
* Bacalhau: Salted cod is a Portuguese staple. You’ll find it prepared in countless ways.
* Sardinhas: If you’re visiting in June, don’t miss the grilled sardines during the Santos Populares festivals.
* Ginjinha: This sour cherry liqueur is a local favourite. Try it in a chocolate cup for an extra treat.
* Vinho Verde: This young, slightly sparkling wine is perfect for warm Lisbon days.

## Where to stay in Lisbon
Choosing where to stay in Lisbon can greatly impact your experience. Here are five great areas to consider:

* Baixa/Chiado: This central area is perfect for first-time visitors. Baixa offers a flat terrain (rare in Lisbon!) with grand squares and pedestrianised shopping streets. Chiado, just uphill, is more upscale with designer shops and historic cafes. Staying here puts you within walking distance of many major attractions.
* Alfama: If you’re after charm and history, Alfama’s your spot. This is Lisbon’s oldest neighbourhood, with narrow winding streets, tiny squares, and fado clubs. It’s hilly and can be a bit of a maze, but that’s part of its charm. Great for those who want to immerse themselves in old Lisbon.
* Bairro Alto: This is the place to be if you’re into nightlife. By day, it’s a quiet residential area. By night, it transforms into Lisbon’s party central, with bars and restaurants spilling onto the streets. It’s centrally located but can be noisy at night, so perhaps not ideal for light sleepers.
* Avenida da Liberdade: Lisbon’s grand boulevard is lined with designer shops, five-star hotels, and beautiful buildings. It’s a bit pricier, but you get a more luxurious experience. It’s great for those who enjoy a central location but prefer a quieter, more upscale area.
* Príncipe Real: This trendy neighbourhood is known for its antique shops, hip restaurants, and beautiful garden. It’s a bit further from the main tourist sites but offers a more local experience. Great for return visitors or those who prefer a more residential feel.
