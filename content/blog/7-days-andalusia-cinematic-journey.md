**By Nodes of Travel**  
*A Week in Andalusia | The Ultimate Southern Spain Itinerary*

## 🎬 Introduction
Tucked away in the south of Spain, the remarkable region of Andalusia feels like several worlds woven into one. Roman bridges stand beside Moorish palaces. Gothic cathedrals rise above ancient Islamic architecture. Whitewashed villages cling dramatically to cliffs, while flamenco music still echoes through streets where it was born centuries ago.

We'll take you through the exact route we followed—from the vibrant streets of Seville to the timeless beauty of Córdoba, the magic of Granada, and finally two of Andalusia's most spectacular white villages: Ronda and Setenil de las Bodegas.
If you're planning your first trip to southern Spain...this itinerary is for you.


We explored this incredible region over six unforgettable days, although if your schedule allows, I'd strongly recommend stretching it into a week. A little extra time lets you slow down and truly enjoy places like Seville and Granada.

-----

## 🇪🇸 DAY 1 — SEVILLE
Every great journey needs the perfect beginning. And for Andalusia... that beginning is Seville.

Lively, colourful and effortlessly beautiful, Seville has a way of slowing you down from the very moment you arrive.

We started our day in Barrio Santa Cruz, the city's enchanting old Jewish Quarter.
It's the kind of neighbourhood where maps become almost irrelevant. Narrow cobbled lanes twist between whitewashed buildings, tiny plazas appear without warning, and almost every corner feels like it belongs on a postcard.

One thing we immediately noticed were Seville's beautiful orange trees.
Lining streets, courtyards and squares across the city, they've become one of Seville's most recognizable symbols, adding colour and character wherever you walk.

 Before long, we arrived at one place we simply couldn't miss... the magnificent Royal Alcázar.
Walking through its elegant courtyards and peaceful gardens, you quickly begin to understand what makes Andalusia so unique.
Throughout the region, you'll notice an extraordinary blend of Islamic artistry and Christian heritage, and few places showcase that harmony more beautifully than the Alcázar.

After a morning filled with history, it was time to experience another side of Seville...
its incredible food. We stopped at the lively Bodegas Santa Cruz, one of those wonderfully authentic tapas bars where locals and visitors gather shoulder to shoulder over small plates, lively conversations and a glass of something refreshing.

Right next door, María Luisa Park offered a completely different atmosphere.
Towering trees, shaded pathways and peaceful fountains made it the perfect place to escape the afternoon heat before our day gradually shifted into evening.

From there, we made our way to what is undoubtedly Seville's most iconic landmark...Plaza de España.
Even after seeing countless photographs beforehand, standing here in person was something else entirely.
The sweeping canal...the colourful ceramic benches...the elegant bridges...
and the sheer scale of the architecture make it one of the most beautiful public squares we've visited anywhere in Europe.

As the golden light softened, we found ourselves strolling along the banks of the Guadalquivir River.
Passing the historic Torre del Oro and crossing the elegant Triana Bridge, the city seemed to slow to an entirely different rhythm.

At Triana cafés began to fill...as the neighbourhood took on a warm, relaxed atmosphere that was impossible not to love.
It was the perfect ending to our first day. And yet... we had barely scratched the surface.

-----
-----

## 🇪🇸 DAY 2 — SEVILLE
The following morning, we returned to Seville's historic heart.
Waiting for us was one of the city's greatest landmarks...Seville Cathedral and its iconic Giralda Tower.

No matter how many pictures you've seen beforehand, nothing quite prepares you for their sheer scale.
Stepping inside, it's impossible not to appreciate the layers of history that have shaped this remarkable city over the centuries.

Afterwards, we simply wandered. Just letting Seville reveal itself one beautiful street at a time.
Sometimes those unplanned moments become the most memorable part of any journey.

By lunchtime, we treated ourselves to one of the highlights of our Spanish adventure...a freshly prepared seafood paella.
In Seville, there was one final experience we couldn't miss. Las Setas.
Officially known as the Metropol Parasol, this remarkable wooden structure offers one of the finest panoramic views across the city.
We timed our visit for late afternoon  and watching the city transform from day into evening felt like the perfect farewell.

## 🇪🇸 DAY 3 — CÓRDOBA
One of the things we loved most about this itinerary was how easy it was to travel between the major cities. 
Spain's high-speed trains made the journey to Córdoba incredibly comfortable, leaving us free to simply enjoy the changing landscapes along the way.

In less than an hour aboard Spain's excellent high-speed train Renfe…we found ourselves arriving in Córdoba.
Córdoba's historic heart consists of Whitewashed streets... flower-filled patios... hidden courtyards... and narrow alleyways that seem designed for wandering rather than rushing. This is the old Jewish Quarter...and honestly... it's one of the most atmospheric neighbourhoods we've visited anywhere in Spain.
We weren't trying to see everything. We simply allowed ourselves to get lost. Sometimes... that's the best itinerary of all.

Calleja de las Flores
Around one corner...we arrived at one of Córdoba's most photographed little streets...Calleja de las Flores. A narrow alley framed by colourful flower pots...ending with a perfect view of the Mezquita's bell tower. It's small...simple... and unmistakably Córdoba.

Bar Santos
Of course... no visit to Córdoba would be complete without trying one of its local favourites.
Just beside the Mezquita sits the legendary Bar Santos...famous for serving enormous slices of Spanish patata tortilla.
Just honest food... done exceptionally well. Sometimes... those are the meals you remember most.


THE MEZQUITA

And then...came the place that draws visitors from around the world. The Mezquita-Cathedral of Córdoba.
We've visited many remarkable buildings while travelling...but very few have left an impression quite like this.
Walking inside...the endless rows of red and white arches seem to stretch on forever.

What makes this place so extraordinary isn't simply its beauty...but the story it tells.
Over centuries, Córdoba became a meeting point of civilizations... where Roman...Islamic...Jewish...and Christian influences all left their mark.
Their stories continue to exist together beneath the same roof.


Before leaving...don't miss climbing the bell tower.
Looking out across Córdoba from above gives you a wonderful perspective of the city you've just explored...the Roman Bridge...
the winding streets...and the rooftops stretching endlessly into the Andalusian countryside.

As the afternoon slowly turned into evening...we found ourselves  beside the river.
The crowds had thinned...the light softened... and Córdoba became even more beautiful.
Watching the Roman Bridge reflected across the Guadalquivir...before the city gradually transformed into night...
became one of the quietest...yet most memorable moments of this entire journey.

Before calling it a day...we enjoyed one final Andalusian meal at Taberna Casa Bravo.
Great food...a welcoming local atmosphere...and the perfect way to end a city that never once tried to overwhelm us...
yet somehow left one of the deepest impressions of the entire trip.

One of the things we loved about this trip is how every city feels completely different from the last.
Just when we thought we understood Andalusia...it surprised us again. Our next stop...Granada.

## 🇪🇸 DAY 4 — GRANADA

If Córdoba feels timeless...Granada feels mysterious.
From the moment we stepped into its streets... there was something different about the atmosphere.
Maybe it was the backdrop of the Sierra Nevada. Maybe it was the unmistakable influence of North Africa.
Or perhaps it was simply knowing that somewhere above the city...
waited one of the greatest architectural masterpieces in the world.
But before discovering the Alhambra...we wanted to experience Granada itself.

Mercado San Agustín.
Like many great travel days...ours began with food. We stopped at the lively Mercado de San Agustín...where fresh seafood, local produce and traditional Andalusian flavours come together beneath one roof.
It's exactly the kind of place we'd recommend if you want to experience the city beyond the usual touristy restaurants.

Cathedral
A short walk later...we arrived at Granada Cathedral.
Elegant...peaceful...and filled with remarkable history.

Just next door lies the Royal Chapel...where Spain's Catholic Monarchs, Ferdinand and Isabella, rest.
Photography isn't allowed inside...but perhaps that's fitting. Some places are simply meant to be experienced rather than photographed.

Ice cream
Afterwards...we made a quick stop at one of Granada's most famous traditions...Los Italianos.
Their iconic Cassata ice cream has become something of a local institution...and after a morning of exploring... it tasted even better than we'd imagined.


Free tapas.
One thing that immediately makes Granada stand apart from every other city on this journey...is its famous free tapas culture.
Order a drink...and your tapas arrive with it. It's a tradition that's become part of Granada's identity...and one we happily embraced at Bar Casa Julio.
It's simple...authentic... and one of the many reasons people fall in love with this city.

There were moments...especially around the tiny cafés and winding lanes... when it genuinely reminded us of Morocco.

Darro River
As the afternoon unfolded...we followed the beautiful Carrera del Darro...often considered one of the most picturesque streets in all of Spain.
With the river flowing quietly beside us...ancient stone bridges...centuries-old buildings...and the Alhambra watching from above...every step felt almost cinematic.

Albaicín
Gradually...the streets began to climb. Whitewashed houses...narrow alleyways...hidden viewpoints...and suddenly...
Granada began to reveal its true character. This is Albaicín...and wandering these streets feels almost like stepping into another world.


Sacromonte & Flamenco
But Granada still had one more unforgettable experience waiting for us. we continued into Sacromonte...
home to Granada's famous cave dwellings and the Romani community whose traditions have shaped flamenco for generations.
Watching a live flamenco performance inside Cueva Zíncale wasn't just entertainment.
It felt deeply personal. Every guitar note... every clap... every movement seemed to carry generations of emotion.
Even without understanding every word... you could feel every moment.



Mirador de San Nicolás
Then...came the view we'd been waiting for. Mirador de San Nicolás.
Standing here...with the Alhambra glowing across the valley and the Sierra Nevada rising beyond...it's easy to understand why this has become one of Spain's most celebrated viewpoints. 

We finished our day back at Plaza Nueva, enjoying one last evening of wonderful tapas before heading back through Granada's softly lit streets.
Tomorrow...the city had saved its greatest masterpiece for last. The Alhambra.

## 🇪🇸 DAY 5 — THE ALHAMBRA
Day Five of our Andalusia journey began with the experience we'd been looking forward to the most...By now, Andalusia had already surprised us more than once...
This morning was dedicated to a place that had inspired this entire trip from the very beginning. The Alhambra.

Perched high above Granada... overlooking the city below... the Alhambra isn't simply Andalusia's greatest landmark.
It's one of Europe's greatest masterpieces. And because entry numbers are carefully controlled, planning ahead is essential.
If you're following this itinerary yourself, be sure to reserve your tickets well in advance—especially for the Nasrid Palaces, where entry is timed and strictly enforced. It's one small piece of planning... that makes all the difference.

Generalife
We began our morning in the Generalife Gardens. Before the crowds arrived... everything felt wonderfully peaceful.
Flowing fountains...shaded pathways...fragrant gardens...and incredible views across Granada.
It's hard to imagine a more beautiful way to begin the day.
There was no rush. We simply wandered... listening to the sound of water that has flowed through these gardens for centuries.

Alcazaba
From there... we continued to the Alcazaba... the oldest part of the Alhambra.
Standing atop its ancient defensive walls...Granada stretched endlessly below us. Looking back towards the Albaicín...
we could trace yesterday's journey through the very streets we'd wandered only hours before.
It's one of those rare moments where a destination suddenly connects with everything you've already experienced.


Palace of Charles V
Right beside the Moorish palaces... another chapter of history quietly unfolds.
The striking Palace of Charles V feels completely different in style... yet somehow belongs here just as naturally.
Throughout Andalusia... that's something you'll notice again and again.
Every civilization leaves its own chapter... and together... they tell one remarkable story.

Nasrid Palaces
And then... it was finally time. The Nasrid Palaces. No photographs...no documentaries…no travel videos... truly prepare you for this place.
Every room seems more intricate than the last. Delicate carvings cover almost every surface.
Courtyards reflect perfect symmetry. 
It's almost impossible to believe that so much beauty could have survived for centuries. 
We took it all in. Some places don't need narration.
They simply need silence. And for us... this was one of them.

Reflection
Looking back...the Alhambra wasn't simply our favourite monument in Andalusia.
It became one of the most extraordinary places we've ever visited. Beautiful... yes.  But more than that...unforgettable.

After several unforgettable hours exploring the complex... we rewarded ourselves with something far simpler.
Fresh churros and thick Spanish hot chocolate. Sometimes... after experiencing somewhere truly extraordinary... that's exactly what you need.

Up until this point... Spain's high-speed trains had carried us effortlessly from city to city.
But now... the journey was about to change. We picked up a rental car... left Granada behind... and headed deep into Andalusia's mountains.

The destination? A place many consider one of Spain's most beautiful towns. Ronda.

## 🇪🇸 DAY 5 EVENING — RONDA
Every now and then... a place appears that makes you stop walking altogether. Ronda was exactly that.Perched dramatically above the El Tajo Gorge... and divided by one of the most spectacular bridges anywhere in Europe...it almost doesn't look real.

And somehow...our hotel offered the most incredible front-row seat imaginable . Waking up to the Puente Nuevo outside our window... with the gorge stretching far below... felt almost surreal.

As the evening light turned golden...we crossed the bridge towards the Mirador de Aldehuela.
Every viewpoint revealed another breathtaking perspective. The bridge... the cliffs... the endless Andalusian countryside.
Dinner that evening was at El Lechuguita. A wonderfully local tapas bar...full of laughter... conversation...and the kind of atmosphere that makes you want to stay a little longer. 

Afterwards... we returned to our balcony overlooking the bridge. For hours... we simply sat there.
Many visitors arrive for a few hours...take their photographs... and leave. But it's after sunset...and again at sunrise... that Ronda truly becomes unforgettable.

-----
-----

## 🇪🇸 DAY 6 — Ronda & Setenil de las Bodegas
Our final morning in Andalusia began before sunrise. Standing near the Arco del Cristo...watching the first light slowly reach the Puente Nuevo...
was one of those travel moments we'll never forget. The town was almost silent.
And for a little while... it felt as though we had Ronda entirely to ourselves.
After breakfast overlooking the bridge... we explored the quieter side of Ronda. The old Moorish streets... the remarkably preserved Arab Baths...
the charming Puente Viejo... and the city's relaxed rhythm offered a completely different perspective from the dramatic views above the gorge.
Even a stroll along La Bola, Ronda's lively shopping street, reminded us that this is still a town where everyday life quietly continues.

SETENIL
And then...one final surprise. Less than half an hour away... lies one of the most extraordinary villages we've ever visited.Setenil de las Bodegas. (Reveal the rock
overhang.) Nothing quite prepares you for Setenil. Here...enormous rock formations don't sit beside the streets. They are the streets. Homes...cafés... restaurants...all built beneath gigantic natural cliffs.

Walking along Cuevas del Sol... before crossing into Cuevas de la Sombra... genuinely feels like stepping into another world.

Before saying goodbye... there was one last local tradition we couldn't resist. Cortadillo Durán.
A simple regional sweet...rich in flavour... and the perfect final taste of Andalusia.

-----
-----

THE FINALE
Looking back...what made this journey so special wasn't simply the places we visited. Every city feels completely different, yet together they tell the story of Andalusia in a way that's difficult to experience by visiting just one place. A region shaped by centuries of civilizations...extraordinary landscapes...remarkable food... and people who welcome you with genuine warmth.

We completed this route over six unforgettable days… But if you have a little more time..stretch it into a week., we honestly think it's one of the most rewarding journeys you can take anywhere in Spain."
